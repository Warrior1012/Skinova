SOURCE:
American Academy of Dermatology - Melanoma Signs and Symptoms
https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/types/common/melanoma/symptoms

Melanoma can appear as a new or changing spot or mole.

Important warning signs include:
A - Asymmetry: one half of a spot does not match the other half.
B - Border: the border may be irregular, scalloped, or poorly defined.
C - Color: the spot may contain multiple colors.
D - Diameter: melanomas are often larger than 6 mm when diagnosed, but
smaller lesions can also occur.
E - Evolving: the spot changes in size, shape, color, or appearance.

A spot that looks different from other spots, changes, itches, bleeds,
or becomes painful should be evaluated by a dermatologist.

Melanoma can occur on different parts of the body, including areas that
receive little sun exposure.

The ABCDE features are warning signs and do not establish a diagnosis.

A dermatologist should evaluate suspicious lesions. A skin biopsy is used
when needed to establish a definitive diagnosis.

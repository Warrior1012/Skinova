American Academy of Dermatology - Basal Cell Carcinoma

Basal cell carcinoma is a type of skin cancer that commonly grows slowly.
It can appear in different forms including a firm raised growth, a scaly
spot or patch, a sore that does not heal or repeatedly returns, a new mark
that resembles a scar, or shiny bumps.

A lesion that grows, bleeds, crusts, repeatedly breaks open, or does not heal
should be evaluated by a dermatologist.

Ultraviolet radiation from sunlight and indoor tanning devices is an important
risk factor for basal cell carcinoma.

People of all skin colors can develop basal cell carcinoma.

A visual image alone cannot establish that a lesion is basal cell carcinoma.
Professional clinical evaluation is required for diagnosis.

A dermatologist can determine whether a suspicious lesion requires further
evaluation. When skin cancer is suspected, biopsy may be required to establish
a definitive diagnosis.
